# WorkKeys Graphic Literacy: research notes and how the mystery maps to it

## About the test

ACT WorkKeys Graphic Literacy is one of the three tests for the ACT WorkKeys
National Career Readiness Certificate (NCRC), together with Applied Math and
Workplace Documents.

- **Format:** 38 items in 55 minutes (computer-based).
- **Scores:** Levels 3 to 7. Level 3 is the least complex and Level 7 the most
  complex. Each level includes the skills of the levels below it.
- **Graphics on the test:** workplace graphics such as tables, bar and line
  graphs, pie charts, flowcharts, floor plans, maps, forms, dashboards,
  gauges and diagrams.
- **How a level is set:** by the complexity of the graphic (simple, moderate,
  high-moderate, highly complex) combined with how many thinking steps the
  task needs. ACT's technical manual gives this example: a one-step task on a
  low-moderate graphic is Level 3, and the same task on a high-moderate
  graphic is Level 4.

## Skills by level

| Level | Graphic | Tasks |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Simple, familiar (basic table, bar graph, simple floor plan) | Find information that is shown directly; identify the next step in a simple process |
| 4 | More data points and variables | Compare data; spot a trend, pattern or relationship; use one graphic to find information in another; make a simple inference or decision |
| 5 | Difficult or unfamiliar; more detail | Find information in a dense graphic; **identify the next or missing step in a process**; draw a conclusion from the data |
| 6 | High-moderate complexity, less common formats | **Compare two or more trends**; **interpret a trend**; **find information in one graphic using information from another** |
| 7 | Highly complex | Everything above, plus **make a reasonable inference or decision from one graphic after finding information in another**; **justify** the inference; **choose and justify the most effective graphic** for a purpose |

Bolded tasks are worded as in the ACT level descriptions quoted in the
sources below. Level 3 to 4 detail comes from test-prep summaries of ACT's
descriptors, so treat that wording as approximate.

review asks the Level 6 and 7 questions, which need two or more graphics at
once, as the test does.

### Test-taking habits the game builds in

- **Read the title, legend, axis labels and units first.** Every chart has
  them, and wrong answers often come from misreading one: block start times
  on the bar charts, kg on the line graph, and votes compared with percent
  on the pie chart.
- **Find the right row or series before reading a value.** The wrong options
  on E2 are deliberately other people's rows.
- **Watch for distractors.** The pie chart is a motive, not evidence. A
  correct graphic chosen for the wrong reason is wrong (R5, option D).
- **Combine graphics.** The hardest questions (R2 to R4) can't be answered
  from one graphic alone.
- Every wrong option has feedback that explains the misreading, and the
  report records whether each item was right on the first try, by level.
